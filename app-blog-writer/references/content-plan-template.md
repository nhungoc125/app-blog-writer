# Content plan template

The plan is the one document the user must approve. Make it decisive (you've been asked to decide), short (readable in about five minutes), and specific to this product. Every piece must connect to a capability in the product brief.

```markdown
# <Product> content plan
Prepared <date> from the <repo name> source code and a review of current search results.

## Positioning
<One sentence: who it helps, the outcome, what makes it different.>
Primary audience: <segment>. Secondary: <segment>.

## Why these topics
<2–4 sentences: what the search landscape looks like, which battles to avoid (e.g. head terms owned by big brands), where this product can win and why.>

## Topic clusters
| Cluster | Product capability it maps to | Audience | Example queries (validate) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## First pieces, in order
| # | Working title | Type | Target query (validate) | Intent | Non-commodity angle | Proof from the product | Links to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Types: post · landing page · comparison/alternative · integration/setup guide · original data report.
Order: high-intent and winnable first; pillar guides early so later posts can link up to them.

## Images
<For each piece, the planned visuals: real screenshot / rebuilt UI mockup / diagram / featured image. Note which screens of the app each mockup is based on.>

## Cadence
<e.g. 1 piece a week for 12 weeks; adjust to capacity. Quality over volume.>

## Claims to confirm
<From the product brief: items content will avoid until confirmed.>

## How we'll measure
Primary: organic signups and qualified leads per page. Leading: impressions, clicks, AI feature visibility (Search Console). Review each piece at 4 and 12 weeks.

## Your approval
Reply "approved" (or with changes) and I'll start with piece #1.
```

## Choosing pieces well

- **Start where intent is high:** "<competitor> alternative", "<category> for <audience>", "how to <job the product does>", integration/setup pages.
- **Avoid head terms** dominated by large brands unless the product has a genuinely unique angle.
- **One page per topic.** Merge near-duplicates; don't plan separate pages for wording variations.
- **Every piece needs a non-commodity angle.** If you can't name what only this product's team can say, drop or rethink the topic.
- **Respect segments.** If the product serves different buyer types (e.g. software providers vs the businesses that use their software), never mix them in one piece.
- **Match capacity.** Suggest fewer, better pieces rather than a long list that won't get done.
