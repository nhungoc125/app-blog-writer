## 1. What changed in 2026

- **Two broad core updates:** March 27 – April 8 and May 21 – June 2, 2026, plus spam updates in March, June, August and September. Google published no new ranking rules with them; its advice remains to make satisfying, people-first content.
- **Google's official "Optimizing for generative AI search" guide** (updated July 2026) says AI Overviews and AI Mode are rooted in core Search ranking and quality systems. AEO/GEO is, from Google's view, still SEO.
- **New eligibility condition:** besides being indexed and eligible for a snippet, a site must be *included* in the "Search generative AI features" setting in Search Console to appear in AI Overviews/AI Mode (included is the default).
- **Generative AI performance report** in Search Console shows how content performs in AI features.
- **FAQ rich results retired** on May 7, 2026 for all sites. FAQPage markup is still valid vocabulary but no longer produces a SERP feature. HowTo rich results were already gone (2023).
- **AI Mode** grew rapidly (Google said at I/O 2026 it passed one billion monthly users). Queries fan out into many sub-searches, so a page can be cited for related questions it answers well — without separate pages for each variant.

## 2. Content principles

These matter more than anything else for both classic results and AI answers.

**Non-commodity content.** Google contrasts commodity content (e.g. "7 Tips for First-Time Homebuyers", common knowledge anyone could write) with non-commodity content that brings expert or first-hand experience beyond common knowledge. For a product company, non-commodity sources include:

- How the product actually solves the problem, step by step, with real screens
- Decisions and trade-offs visible in the code ("we limit guides to menus and links so users always confirm the final action")
- Patterns from the product's own anonymized data (only with the user's permission)
- Honest comparisons with alternatives, including where they're better
- Lessons from support tickets, onboarding drop-off, real customer questions

**People-first.** Write for the reader's goal; ask "would a visitor feel satisfied after reading this?" Use paragraphs, sections and headings that make the page easy to follow.

**Accuracy.** Generative models predict words; they don't retrieve facts. Google says to fact-check AI-assisted content before publishing — including titles, meta descriptions, structured data and alt text.

**Context about creation.** Where it makes sense for the audience, say how content was made (author, review, use of automation). Name real authors with relevant experience.

**Images and video.** AI features can surface images and video too; high-quality, relevant media gives a page more ways to appear.

## 3. What not to do

- **Don't create pages for every keyword or fan-out variation.** Google explicitly calls this scaled content abuse when done to manipulate rankings or AI answers. One strong page per topic.
- **Don't mass-generate posts** with AI without adding value; it falls under the scaled content abuse policy.
- **Ignore AEO/GEO "hacks" for Google:** no llms.txt needed (Google Search ignores it), no special AI schema, no "chunking" content into fragments, no rewriting just for AI, no buying or faking mentions.
- **Don't overfocus on structured data.** It's not required for AI features; use it where it accurately describes visible content and earns rich results.
- **Don't trust tools claiming "internal Google metrics".** No third-party tool has access to Google's ranking systems.
- **No invented facts:** statistics, customers, testimonials, awards, review ratings.

## 4. On-page checklist

| Element | Guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Title tag | Unique, descriptive, ~50–60 characters, main topic near the start; brand at the end if room |
| H1 | One per page; usually matches or closely mirrors the title |
| Opening | Answer the core question in the first 2–3 sentences |
| Headings | Descriptive H2/H3 in logical order; written for people |
| Meta description | ~140–160 characters; states the benefit; not a keyword list (Google may rewrite it) |
| Slug | Short, lowercase, hyphenated, descriptive; no dates unless content is dated by nature |
| Internal links | Link to 1–3 related pages/posts with descriptive anchor text; link back from older related posts |
| External links | Cite primary sources you actually opened |
| Author | Real name, short bio, relevant experience; author page if possible |
| Dates | Visible published and updated dates; update content meaningfully, not just the date |
| Length | No ideal length — cover the topic fully, cut filler |
| CTA | One relevant next step tied to the product |

## 5. Structured data

Use JSON-LD that matches what's visible on the page.

- `BlogPosting` or `Article`: headline, description, image, datePublished, dateModified, author (Person with url), publisher (Organization with logo)
- `BreadcrumbList` for blog > category > post
- `Organization` on the homepage/about page; `SoftwareApplication` on product/pricing pages where accurate
- `FAQPage`: optional, only for genuine visible Q&A; no rich result since May 2026
- Validate with the Rich Results Test / Schema Markup Validator

Example:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "BlogPosting",
  "headline": "How to Get More SaaS Trial Signups From Your Pricing Page",
  "description": "The five questions buyers ask before signing up, and how to answer them on the page.",
  "image": ["https://example.com/blog/images/saas-pricing-page-trial-signups.webp"],
  "datePublished": "2026-11-30",
  "dateModified": "2026-11-30",
  "author": {"@type": "Person", "name": "Jane Doe", "url": "https://example.com/team/jane"},
  "publisher": {"@type": "Organization", "name": "Example", "logo": {"@type": "ImageObject", "url": "https://example.com/logo.png"}}
}
```

## 6. Images and video

- Use real, relevant images near the text they illustrate; descriptive file names (`guide-me-in-app-guidance.webp`)
- Alt text describes the image's content and purpose in plain language; don't stuff keywords
- Serve WebP/AVIF, set width and height, lazy-load below the fold, keep sizes modest (aim under ~200 KB)
- Provide an Open Graph image (1200 × 630) and Twitter card image
- Consider an image sitemap for image-heavy blogs
- For AI-generated images, IPTC `DigitalSourceType` = `TrainedAlgorithmicMedia` metadata is good practice (required for Merchant Center product images)
- Short demo videos with a transcript give another way to appear

## 7. Technical requirements

- Page must be indexable and eligible for a snippet (no `noindex`/`nosnippet`); robots.txt and CDN must let Googlebot in
- Site included in Search Console's generative AI setting (default)
- One canonical URL per page; correct `rel=canonical`; hreflang for language versions
- Important content available as text (not only in images or client-side code that fails to render)
- Good page experience: fast, stable, mobile-friendly, main content clearly distinguishable
- Reduce duplicate URLs; submit XML sitemap; verify in Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools

## 8. AI search beyond Google

- Other AI assistants use their own crawlers. Search/retrieval bots (e.g. OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot) affect whether a page can be cited; training bots (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended token) affect model training. Blocking one doesn't block the other. Check each company's crawler docs before editing robots.txt.
- Bing's index powers Microsoft Copilot; submit sitemaps to Bing Webmaster Tools too.
- Consistent product descriptions across your site and listings (G2, Capterra, Product Hunt) help assistants describe the product accurately. Listings don't guarantee recommendations.

## 9. Measurement

- Primary: organic signups, qualified leads, paid conversions by landing page
- Leading signals: impressions and clicks per page (Search Console), Generative AI performance report, indexed pages
- Review each piece at ~4 weeks (indexed? impressions?) and ~12 weeks (clicks, signups); improve or consolidate
